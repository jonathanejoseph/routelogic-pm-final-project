# Strategic Product Thinking, Module 1 Lab (Exercise 2)

## Responses
- **Chosen path, which scenario are you committing to? (StreamLine or RouteLogic, or your own):** RouteLogic
- **Strategic crisis, based on the brief, if the company does nothing for 12 months, what happens to its market position?:** If we do nothing for 12 months, the company will keep winning procurement checklists on feature breadth while losing the people who actually use the product daily. Enterprise buyers renew based on capability comparisons, but coordinators are the ones who determine whether the tool gets trusted during time-critical moments. As leaner rivals win coordinators' loyalty on the ground, churn will accelerate at the next renewal cycle — and it'll get misattributed internally to "pricing" or "competitor features" instead of the real cause: platform complexity nobody addressed.
- **Moment of misery, what is the user likely doing outside the product (Google, spreadsheets, a competitor) because your tool isn’t serving them?:** The user is currently forced to bypass RouteLogic entirely during urgent reroutes — keeping a side spreadsheet or calling dispatch directly by phone — because navigating four or five screens (search, filter, confirm, override default, re-confirm) is too slow when a driver's stuck and a delivery window is closing. The workaround isn't occasional; it's become their default move under pressure.
- **Problem hook, summarize the business risk and user pain into one urgent sentence.:** We must solve enterprise churn to leaner, faster rivals by addressing the ten seconds coordinators can't afford to lose navigating RouteLogic during a live, time-critical reroute.
- **Value proposition, based on the proposed initiative (Spotlight or Velocity), what is the new value and why is it urgent to launch now?:** For frontline coordinators making time-critical routing calls, we will collapse the everyday, high-stakes actions into their fastest possible path, because the next renewal won't be decided by the buyer who saw the feature list — it'll be decided by the coordinator who already gave up on the tool.

## Cold-read your own hook
- **Is the business risk high enough to justify a new initiative, a high-stakes threat, or a minor inconvenience?:** High-stakes. This isn't friction that annoys people; it's friction that gets the product bypassed at the exact moment it's supposed to prove its value. That's the kind of gap that shows up as churn, not as a support ticket.
- **Is the moment of misery a systemic problem or just an edge case?:** Systemic, if reroutes are a routine part of the coordinator's job rather than a rare event — worth confirming with real usage data before final commit. If urgent reroutes happen daily or weekly, this is core-workflow failure, not an edge case. That's the one gap in this draft I'd want to close before treating it as settled.
- **Does the value proposition actually remove the obstacle you identified, or is it a generic feature?:** Mostly removes it, but "collapse into the fastest possible path" is still a little abstract — it names the outcome, not the mechanism. Worth tightening in a later pass to something concrete ("one-tap override" or "single-screen confirm") once the audit identifies exactly where the five screens can become one.
